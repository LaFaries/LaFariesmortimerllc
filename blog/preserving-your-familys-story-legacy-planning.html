Preserving Your Family's Story: Why Legacy Planning Isn't Just for the Wealthy

LaFaries Mortimer LLC · 3 min read · September 2026

When most people hear the word "legacy," they think of wealth — trusts, estates, and attorneys. So they assume legacy planning is something for other people.

I see it differently.

What legacy really is

Legacy is not only what you leave to your family. It is what you leave in them — their history, their records, their story, and their faith.

As a family historian, I have spent years tracing family lines and preserving records. And I have learned that the most painful loss is often not money. It is information: the documents no one can find, the stories no one wrote down, and the names no one remembers.

What gets lost when no one plans

What families lose
  • Birth, marriage, death, and military records scattered or missing
  • Property deeds and insurance policies no one knew existed
  • Passwords and accounts that lock out the family
  • Family stories that disappear with the elders who carried them
  • Photos with no names, dates, or places attached

Five steps every family can take — at any income

  • Gather vital records. Birth certificates, marriage certificates, Social Security cards, military discharge papers, and death certificates for each generation.
  • Create a family document vault. Scan important documents into secure, organized digital storage — and make sure at least one trusted person knows how to access it.
  • Write down where things are. A simple "If something happens to me" letter listing accounts, policies, and key contacts can spare your family months of searching.
  • Record the elders. Sit down with parents and grandparents. Ask about where they grew up, who their people were, and what they want remembered. Record it.
  • Label and organize photos. Names, dates, and places turn a box of pictures into a family archive.

Legacy is a ministry, too

Scripture speaks of telling the next generation what the Lord has done. Preserving family history is part of that charge. When our children and grandchildren know where they came from, they understand who they are — and Whose they are.

Start with what you have

You do not need a perfect plan to begin. You need a starting point. One folder. One recorded conversation. One labeled photo album. Structure turns good intentions into something your family can actually hold.
