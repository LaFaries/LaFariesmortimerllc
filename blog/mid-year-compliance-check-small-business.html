The Mid-Year Compliance Check Every Small Business Should Run

LaFaries Mortimer LLC · 3 min read · July 2026

Most business owners only think about compliance twice a year: when something is due, and when something goes wrong.

The problem is that compliance issues rarely announce themselves. A lapsed registered agent, an expired license, or a missed filing can sit quietly for months — until a bank, a client, or a state agency notices first.

The principle

Compliance is not a once-a-year event. It is a rhythm.

That is why I recommend every small business, nonprofit, and ministry run a simple mid-year compliance check. July is ideal: the first half of the year is behind you, and there is still plenty of time to fix anything before year-end.

Your mid-year compliance checklist

  • Check your status with the state. Search your business on your Secretary of State (in Florida, Sunbiz) and confirm it shows as active. If your annual report was missed, act now — in Florida, annual reports are due between January 1 and May 1.
  • Confirm your registered agent. Is the person or service still correct, and are they actually forwarding your notices?
  • Review licenses and permits. Local business tax receipts, professional licenses, and industry permits each have their own renewal dates.
  • Update ownership and officer records. If a member, manager, director, or officer changed this year, your state records should reflect it.
  • Stay on top of estimated taxes. If you pay quarterly, the next federal estimated payments typically fall in mid-September and mid-January.
  • Organize contractor information. Collect W-9s now from anyone you pay, so January 1099 season is not a scramble.
  • Review insurance coverage. Make sure your policies still match what your business actually does today.
  • Confirm your banking and records are separate. Mixing personal and business funds weakens the liability protection your entity is meant to provide.

For nonprofits and churches

Tax-exempt organizations have additional responsibilities. Confirm your exempt status is in good standing, know which annual IRS information return (if any) your organization is required to file, keep board minutes current, and make sure donation acknowledgments are being issued properly.

What happens when no one is checking

The cost of waiting
  • Administrative dissolution by the state for missed reports
  • Late fees and reinstatement costs
  • Frozen bank accounts or delayed loans
  • Lost eligibility for grants and contracts
  • Personal liability exposure for owners

None of these happen overnight. They happen because no one owned the calendar.

Make compliance a system, not a memory

The goal is not to become a compliance expert. The goal is to have a system that remembers for you. That is exactly why LAAP Suite™ includes compliance dashboards, automated reminders, and a document vault — so deadlines are tracked before they become emergencies.

This article is general guidance, not legal or tax advice. Deadlines and requirements vary by state and entity type — confirm specifics with your state agency or a qualified professional.
